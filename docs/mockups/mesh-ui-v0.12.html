WHAT CHANGES · v0.12

  1. Header actions like Lab and Sweep. Open recipe, Add part, Queue and Open in F13LD.lab move up as drawn-icon buttons; the F13LD mark doubles as the busy status (it ripples while working).
  2. View tabs. Design, Print (build plate with overhang colours) and Section (cut plane, same as the design tools) instead of buttons scattered over the viewport. Preview quality and the view menu sit at the right of the strip.
  3. Scene rail. Recipes and parts live in one left column: compact family-coloured cards with details on hover, parts grouped by weld. Replaces the bottom drop strip and chip row.
  4. Bigger viewport. Only the family badge, gimbal and a small info chip float over the model.
  5. Inspector shows numbers only. Estimate, Measured (after export, with the field · mesh · simplify split) and Homogenization. No form fields on the right any more.
  6. Dock + Configure drawer. Every setting moves into a drawer with three tabs — Part prep, Structure, Export — exactly like Lab. The dock tags summarise them; click a tag to jump to its tab.
  7. Export is the primary action at the bottom right with its estimate beside it, and a progress strip with stages and Cancel above the dock while it runs.
  8. Mesh colour. Tool name, active tabs and the Export button use the Mesh brand pair (tile #FCEAF5, stroke #6B0A4C) with a lighter on-dark accent, as Lab does with its red.
  9. Phone. One column: viewport first, rails below, the dock stays at the bottom, the drawer opens as a sheet. No horizontal scrolling anywhere.
  10. Structure is edited next to the view, not in the drawer. Select a part and its editor opens beside the viewport (right column by default; try the other two placements below). Every slider updates the preview live.
  11. Each part owns its fill. Solid, Lattice, or Lattice + skin, with its own recipe, cell size, wall offset, rotation and shift — the same recipe can fill two parts at different cell sizes. Drag a recipe card onto a part to fill it.
  12. Welds are objects you can select. Click the weld header to edit how its parts join: fillet radius, and a join rule — Union (plain fillet), Anchor (lattice runs into the solid by a set depth so it bonds) or Graded (walls thicken toward the solid over a set distance). Show joins highlights the contact zone.
