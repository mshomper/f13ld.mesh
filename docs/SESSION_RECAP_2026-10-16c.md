# Session recap — 2026-10-16 (c) · v0.15.0 parallel slab mesher

Branch `slab-mesher`. Matt's decisions this session: the mesher switch is a **visible setting**; volume may differ from the classic mesher by up to 1–2 %; welds come in a second step after he has click-tested single parts; every export gets a **loose-piece clean-up** that splits the mesh and drops junk pieces — default **Remove specks** (pieces under 10 mesh cells), plus **Keep all** and **Largest piece only**. Reaction-diffusion and other grains can be woven or disconnected by design, so the clean-up must never be relative to the largest piece by default.

## What shipped

- `worker/m34-slab-mesher.js` — our own marching tetrahedra on exactly the grid `Manifold.levelSet` samples (`weldGridDims`, m31). Tets are the BCC tetrahedralisation (two neighbouring cell centres + one edge of their shared face); every tet covers three layers L..L+2 and a slab owns tets with L in [La, Lb). Edges in layers Lb, Lb+1 are shared with the next slab: their crossings are interpolated from the owner end with identical arithmetic, reported with a global key (point id · 8 + slot), and `joinSlabs` welds them. Values on the outer grid layer are clamped ≤ 0 and positions clamped to the box (caps land exactly on the box faces, as levelSet). Winding comes from each tet pattern's handedness (`BASE`, 12 patterns). Each crossing is held at least 0.01 % of an edge from both ends of its edge (`SLAB_TCLAMP`), so no two vertices share a position (TPMS and beam fields have grid points exactly on the surface) and the topology comes only from the signs — the raw mesh is always a closed 2-manifold. Snapping crossings onto grid points (`SLAB_SNAP`, off) was tried at 10 % and 0.1 %: both merge vertices and made edges shared by four triangles (thin walls; the shape-clipped beam case). The one merge that stays: a crossing landing exactly on a box-face point clamped to 0 is that point (`capMerge`), which gives levelSet's clean caps (without it, near-duplicate cap vertices made meshopt tear the closed Kelvin foam's caps). Each slab also meshes, without keeping them, the neighbouring slabs' tets that touch the shared layers (two layers each side) and returns the seam-vertex pairs they join (`nbrA` / `nbrB`): only those edges must come out of the slab's simplify unchanged; any other edge between seam vertices is the slab's own and just has to close. Values stream through a rolling window of layers (halo 2), so memory per slab is a few layers.
- `worker/m35-slab-cleanup.js` — `safeSimplify` (meshopt with per-vertex locks; if the result isn't closed, or an open (seam) edge changed, lock the tear and rings around it on a schedule (1,1,2,2,3,3,4,6,8,10,12,12 — tears can creep a cell per retry along flat walls that pass through grid planes) and redo, up to 12 times; a changed shared seam edge locks one ring; locks add up; gives up to the unsimplified input), `simplifySlab` (seam vertices locked, plus the triangles holding an open seam edge), `simplifySeamBands` (second pass over triangles within 4 edges of each seam, outer edges locked), `meshClosedReport` (every directed edge once with its reverse; sort-based), `removeLoosePieces(vp, tv, mode, cellVol)` (`specks` < 10 cells, `largest` keeps the biggest solid piece and the cavities inside it (ray test), `keep`; negative-volume pieces are sealed cavities and are kept unless under one cell).
- `worker/m36-slab-pool.js` — the jobs (≈3 per worker, ≥ 24 layers each) go to `field-bake-worker.js` (new `mesh` job); under 150 k points or one core it runs as one slab in the mesh worker. Results join in slab order, so the output doesn't depend on worker timing.
- `worker/m90-onmessage.js` — open-cube and shape-clipped fine pass use the slab mesher unless `d.mesher==='classic'`; any error or a result that isn't closed falls back to classic (`slabFallback`, shown in the report). The shape coarse pass is still Manifold (64× fewer points). Welds stay classic. Classic simplify is unchanged (byte-identical); its open edges are counted and shown as a warning chip.
- Export tab (`56-dock.js`): **Mesher** (Slab · every core / Classic · Manifold) and **Loose pieces** (Remove specks (default) / Keep all / Largest piece only), stored in `localStorage` (`f13ld.mesh.mesher`, `f13ld.mesh.loose`, helpers in `60-export-ui.js`); the worker reads `d.mesher` and `d.looseMode`. The estimate has its own learned key per mesher (`…:slab`).

## Findings

- **The classic path ships torn meshes on thin walls.** Manifold splits pinch points into duplicate vertices where walls are about one edge thick; meshoptimizer then opens them (thousands of open edges on a thin gyroid sheet; Draft exports of the noise, grain and shape-tpms harness cases hit it). Smaller tolerances don't help. The slab mesher doesn't make those duplicates, so it stays closed.
- Wet bimodal foam reads +3 % against classic: classic's file is torn (2,682 open edges); at half the edge classic gives 38.8 vs slab 35.9 vs classic 34.9 at Med, so the slab result is the closer one (thin Plateau borders).
- Volume: the slab mesher is closer to exact than Manifold on the sphere and gyroid (e.g. gyroid solid at 0.2: −0.25 % vs −0.40 %; sheet at 0.1: within 0.2 %).
- Speed on one core (Node): gyroid 8.4 M points 3.6 s vs Manifold 7.4 s; sphere 9.4 M points 3.6 s vs 3.0 s (cheap field, our per-point overhead shows). Multi-core it divides by the slab count.
